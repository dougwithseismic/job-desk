# Writing the CV

## The truth rule

Every title, employer, date, degree, number and skill comes from the candidate. Rewording is fine; inventing isn't. If a bullet would be stronger with a number, ask for the number. If they don't know it, write the bullet without one.

## Bullets

- Start with what they did, as a verb: led, built, cut, launched, ran, wrote, sold.
- Then the result, with a number where there is one: "chargeback losses fell 38% in nine months".
- One or two lines. Three to five bullets for recent roles, one or two for older ones.
- Bold only the one number that matters most, at most once per bullet.
- No "responsible for", "duties included", "helped with", or first person ("I", "my").
- No buzzwords: results-driven, dynamic, passionate, synergy, go-getter, team player, detail-oriented.

## Headline and summary

- **Headline:** the role they want, plainly: "Senior product manager, payments and risk". No adjectives.
- **Summary:** two to four sentences: what they do, for how long, where, and one proof point. It may be in the first person, sparingly. Tailor it for every application.

## Skills

Grouped (three to five groups), specific (tools, methods, domains), no soft-skill lists, no rating bars.

## International conventions

- No photo, date of birth, marital status, nationality (unless it's about work rights), religion or full address. City and country is enough.
- Phone with the country code. Dates as "Mar 2022".
- A4 everywhere except the US and Canada (Letter). In the US say "resume" in conversation; the document itself doesn't need a title.
- Work rights (`basics.workRights`) only when they're applying across borders and it helps ("EU citizen, no sponsorship needed").
- Languages with a level they'd stand behind (native, fluent, professional, conversational, or CEFR A1 to C2).
- Spelling follows the market: British English for the UK, Europe and most of the world; American English for the US.

## Length

One page for most people with under about ten years of experience; two pages at most. The renderer reports how full the pages are. Cut the oldest and least relevant detail first, then switch `meta.density` to `compact`.
